 H3: Is It Live, or Is It a Replay?

The most common misrepresentation in Indian online AI education: courses marketed as "live" that are recordings with a TA in chat. ## 🧱 SECTION 4 — H2: The 2026 AI Skill Stack — What a Complete Online AI Course Must Cover

Seven layers. For each: topics, why it matters, and what most Indian online courses skip. This doubles as the reader's audit checklist for any course.

Layer 1 — Foundations. Python for AI, NumPy, pandas, data wrangling, SQL, Git/GitHub, Jupyter/Colab, linear algebra and calculus intuition, probability, statistics, hypothesis testing. Everything above collapses without it; commonly rushed for the career-switchers who need it most.

Layer 2 — Core Machine Learning. Supervised and unsupervised learning, regression, classification, trees, ensembles (random forest, gradient boosting, XGBoost), clustering, dimensionality reduction, feature engineering, cross-validation, bias-variance, regularisation, evaluation metrics, imbalanced data. Most production AI in Indian companies is still classical ML; commonly taught without evaluation rigour.

Layer 3 — Deep Learning. Neural network fundamentals, backpropagation, optimisers, CNNs, RNNs/LSTMs, transformers and attention, transfer learning, PyTorch/TensorFlow, GPU training, training dynamics. You cannot understand LLMs without transformers; commonly reduced to theory with no real training runs.

Layer 4 — Applied AI Domains. NLP (tokenisation, embeddings, classification, NER, sequence models), computer vision (classification, detection, segmentation), time series, recommendation systems, speech basics. This is what job descriptions ask for; commonly CV or NLP is dropped entirely to save weeks.

Layer 5 — Generative AI, LLMs & Agents (the 2026 differentiator). How LLMs work, tokens and embeddings, prompt engineering basic → advanced, LLM APIs (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google), open-weight models (Llama, Mistral, Qwen, Gemma), vector databases, RAG basic → production, chunking and re-ranking, fine-tuning (SFT, LoRA, QLoRA, DPO), AI agents, multi-agent orchestration, agent frameworks (LangGraph, CrewAI, AutoGen, OpenAI Agents SDK), MCP and tool integration, multi-modal AI, LLM evaluation, guardrails. 2026 hiring growth concentrates here; commonly half-covered — prompting and one API call, then stop.

Layer 6 — Production (MLOps & LLMOps). Model packaging, FastAPI/Flask serving, Docker, CI/CD basics, experiment tracking (MLflow/W&B), model registry, monitoring and drift detection, cost and latency optimisation, cloud deployment, LLM observability, prompt versioning, evaluation pipelines. The largest gap between "trained a model" and "employable"; commonly skipped or reduced to one lecture.

Layer 7 — Professional. Portfolio construction, GitHub hygiene and READMEs, technical communication, AI system design, case-study interview practice, ethics and responsible AI, governance awareness, domain application thinking. Capability you can't demonstrate doesn't convert into offers; commonly reduced to a resume template.

> The Seven-Layer Audit: Before paying for any course — including any in this list — take its syllabus PDF and mark which layers it covers hands-on, which it covers as theory, and which it skips. If Layer 5 is only prompting, or Layer 6 is absent, you're looking at a 2023 course wearing a 2026 label.

Step 3 — Be honest about discipline. If you've abandoned two or more self-paced courses, that's evidence — push toward live cohort formats regardless of price sensitivity. Frame it without judgement: structure is a tool, not a character verdict.

Step 4 — Set your real budget, including the cost of not finishing: fee + GST + EMI interest + cloud/API credits + opportunity cost of hours. Expected cost = fee ÷ probability you finish. A ₹30,000 course you have a 30% chance of finishing costs more in expectation than a ₹80,000 course you have a 90% chance of finishing.

Step 5 — The 12-question pre-enrollment checklist (screenshot-able): 1) Is the class genuinely live, and can I observe one? 2) Who teaches my batch, and what's their industry background? 3) What's the doubt-resolution SLA, and what if it's missed? 4) Does a human review my code? 5) When was the curriculum last updated, and which modules changed? 6) Does it include production RAG, fine-tuning, agents and MLOps? 7) Do I design projects or follow along? 8) Is anything deployed? 9) What's the refund policy in writing, with the exact cut-off date? 10) Is the EMI a bank loan that continues if I stop attending? 11) What does "placement assistance" include, item by item? 12) Can I speak to two alumni from the last six months you didn't hand-pick?

SECTION 12 — H2: Red Flags — Spotting a Bad Online AI Course Before You Pay

1. Guaranteed job or salary claims — guarantees are usually conditional to the point of meaninglessness. 2. Refusal to share a module-level syllabus before payment. 3. "Live" that turns out to be recordings. 4. No last-updated date on the curriculum; in AI, undated means outdated. 5. No RAG, agents, fine-tuning or MLOps in a 2026 syllabus. 6. "10+ projects" with no descriptions. 7. Manufactured scarcity — "price goes up tonight." 8. Testimonials without full names, companies or LinkedIn profiles. 9. Placement statistics with no denominator. 10. Instructor names withheld until after enrollment. 11. No refund policy, or a window shorter than the first module. 12. EMI through a lender whose terms you can't see before signing. 13. A curriculum that's 70% classical ML with a GenAI cover slide. 14. Certificates presented as the primary outcome. 15. No mechanism for human feedback on your code.

On sales calls: get everything in writing, never pay on the same call, and treat urgency as information about the seller, not the offer.
